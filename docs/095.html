Appendix 7　Chronology

This chronology sets three lines in a single table: the history of the Luminous Religion itself; great events in the Tang and the wider world; and the story of this book. The whole table runs in order of time, from the Church of the East becoming independent in the fifth century to the common declaration of the twentieth. The first two columns are history; the "In the book" column is the novel's plot, with the book and chapter in brackets so that readers can look them up. The dates and deeds of the book's characters, except where they are recorded on the stele or in the histories, are mostly invented, and readers should not take them as fact.

Years are given AD first, with the reign period in its own column; reign periods after the Tang name the dynasty. Events spanning years are placed under the year they began; those marked "c." are placed under the most likely year.

Notes on sources

  • The "In the book" column: from the text of this book; book and chapter numbers are given in the table.
  • "The world" column: the Tang entries come from 3, 4 and 5, and can be checked against standard chronologies of Tang history.
  • "Luminous Religion" column, 635–781: from 1 and 2. For Surin's usurpation in 753 and the career of Timothy I, see the sources listed in Appendix 8.
  • The year of discovery, 1623–1625, is disputed: the Forest of Stelae gives the third year of Tianqi (20); Western works mostly give 1625 (18, 21).
  • The Book of Heraclides, 1889: see the bibliography of Appendix 6.
  • Doubtful items:
  • "c. 486, the Church of the East adopts the Antiochene theological tradition" refers to the synod of Beth Lapat in 484 and the synod of Acacius in 486; exact years to be checked (13).
  • Patriarchs' dates in 750–800 differ by a year or two between authorities (18).
