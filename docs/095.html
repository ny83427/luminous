Appendix 7　Chronology

This chronology sets three lines side by side: the history of the Luminous Religion itself; great events in the Tang and the wider world; and the story of this book. The first two columns are history; the third is the novel's plot, with chapter numbers in brackets. The dates and deeds of the book's characters, except where they are recorded on the stele or in the histories, are mostly invented, and readers should not take them as fact.

Years are given AD, with the Tang reign period after them.

2. Book One: Alaha (the book of Yisi)

AD Luminous Religion The world In the book
697 Guo Ziyi born
725 Yisi born in Balkh in Tokharistan; his father Milis a priest
742 1st year of Tianbao Yisi, seventeen, leaves Balkh and comes to the Daqin monastery in the Yining Ward of Chang'an (Ch. 3)
744–746 Yonan, bishop of Chang'an, rescues A-Lian in the alley behind the West Market and teaches her to read (Ch. 37)
746 5th year of Tianbao Jingjing born, son of Yonan and A-Lian, adopted by Yisi and his wife
748 7th year of Tianbao Yisi's father Milis dies in Balkh; the news reaches Chang'an only the next year (Ch. 3)
c. 752 Yisi's wife dies of illness
753 Surin bribes the local governor and is forcibly consecrated patriarch, soon deposed; Jacob II succeeds Yisi's elder brother Shapur, caught in the struggle at the Patriarchate, is attacked and loses his right arm
754 13th year of Tianbao Winter: Yisi's mother dies in Balkh; the news reaches Lingzhou more than a year later (Ch. 3)
755 Winter of the 14th year of Tianbao: An Lushan rises at Fanyang Yisi saves people at a small town by the Yellow River and is taken into Guo Ziyi's service (Ch. 2)
756 15th year of Tianbao / 1st year of Zhide. Tong Pass falls, Xuanzong flees to Shu, Suzong takes the throne at Lingwu Jingjing travels west to the Patriarchate (Ch. 3); Yisi holds Changshan with Li Guangbi (Chs. 5, 6); Du De dies in the rearguard (Ch. 8)
756–757 Jingjing reaches Ctesiphon, returns east, and stays in Xizhou (Chs. 9–13)
757 2nd year of Zhide. Fourth month, defeat at the Qing Canal; ninth month, battle of Xiangji Temple and recovery of Chang'an; tenth month, recovery of Luoyang. Uyghur and Arab troops take part Guo Ziyi secretly baptised with a bronze basin in the camp (Ch. 15); at Xiangji Temple Yisi is hit by three arrows (Ch. 18); entry into Chang'an (Ch. 20)
758 1st year of Qianyuan Gruel given out in Chang'an in groups of fifty (Ch. 22); polo (Ch. 23); Yisi granted the purple robe (Ch. 24)
759 Third month of the 2nd year of Qianyuan: the nine military governors routed at Xiangzhou Old Duan dies in battle (Ch. 27)
760 1st year of Shangyuan The trouble with "the Pure Ones" (Ch. 28)
761–762 The new city of Baghdad begun Jingjing goes again to the Patriarchate to report (Ch. 30); Shapur assassinated (Ch. 31)
763 1st year of Guangde. The An Lushan rebellion ends; the eunuch Luo Fengxian slanders Pugu Huai'en Three elders come to Chang'an with Jingjing to pass on the scriptures (Ch. 32)
764 2nd year of Guangde. Pugu Huai'en's officers kill Hun Shizhi at Lingzhou Ch. 34
765 1st year of Yongtai. Pugu Huai'en leads Tibetans and Uyghurs in invasion; dies of illness at Mingsha in the ninth month; in the tenth month Guo Ziyi rides alone to meet the Uyghurs Chs. 35, 36; end of Book One

AD Luminous Religion The world In the book
from 766 Dali years Jingjing directs the Chinese translation of scriptures (Ch. 38)
773 Ḥnanīšōʿ II (the stele's "Ningshu") becomes patriarch
775 The patriarch's seat moves from Ctesiphon to Baghdad 10th year of Dali Yonan and A-Lian meet again (Ch. 39)

778–780 Hnanisho II dies; Timothy I elected by manoeuvre, his opponents unreconciled Ch. 44
779 14th year of Dali. Daizong dies, Dezong takes the throne Yisi proposes a stele (Ch. 45)
781 Seventh day of the first month: the Luminous stele raised in Chang'an, composed by Jingjing, written by Lü Xiuyan; the Syriac still names the late Hnanisho as patriarch 2nd year of Jianzhong. Sixth month, Guo Ziyi dies Chs. 47–49
782 Timothy I debates religion with the caliph al-Mahdi
783–784 4th year of Jianzhong, mutiny of the Jingyuan troops, Dezong flees to Fengtian; 1st year of Xingyuan, Chang'an recovered The Daqin monastery shut for eight months; in autumn Yisi's old wounds flare up and he dies (Ch. 50)
786 The north Indian monk Prajñā and Jingjing translate the Sutra of the Six Pāramitās; it is condemned as "obscure in sense and loose in wording," and an edict orders that "Jingjing should transmit the teaching of Mishihe" 2nd year of Zhenyuan Ch. 51
c. 795 Jingfu born, son of a Sogdian soldier of the Shence army and a Chinese mother
805 Mi Jifen, an officer of the Shence army and a Luminous believer, dies 1st year of Yongzhen Ch. 52
c. 800s Timothy I consecrates metropolitans for the Turks, China and elsewhere Timothy's letter (Ch. 53)
814 A Luminous scripture pillar raised in Luoyang 9th year of Yuanhe Mentioned in Ch. 55
817 Li Su, a Persian Luminous believer and director of the Bureau of Astronomy, dies 12th year of Yuanhe Mentioned in Ch. 38
818 13th year of Yuanhe Jingjing dies at seventy-two, handing the lamp at the gate to Jingfu (Ch. 54)
823 Timothy I dies

Notes on sources

  • The "In the book" column: from the text of this book; chapter numbers are given in the table.
  • "The world" column: the Tang entries come from 3, 4 and 5, and can be checked against standard chronologies of Tang history.
  • "Luminous Religion" column, 635–781: from 1 and 2. For Surin's usurpation in 753 and the career of Timothy I, see the sources listed in Appendix 8.
  • The year of discovery, 1623–1625, is disputed: the Forest of Stelae gives the third year of Tianqi (20); Western works mostly give 1625 (18, 21).
  • The Book of Heraclides, 1889: see the bibliography of Appendix 6.
  • Doubtful items:
  • "c. 486, the Church of the East adopts the Antiochene theological tradition" refers to the synod of Beth Lapat in 484 and the synod of Acacius in 486; exact years to be checked (13).
  • Patriarchs' dates in 750–800 differ by a year or two between authorities (18).
