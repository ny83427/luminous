Luminous

Appendix 2　The Stele, II: Text and Translation

Appendix 1 gave an overview of the stele; this appendix sets out the whole inscription. The left column is the original Chinese, the right column an English translation (on a phone the two are stacked), with brief notes where needed. For the Syriac at the foot and sides of the stele, only a Latin-letter transliteration (abridged) and a translation are given; the transliteration follows the standard scholarly editions and is not a letter-for-letter facsimile. Yisi's passage is discussed in detail in Appendix 3, and the other people in Appendix 4.

The English is the translator's own, made for this book. It aims to be readable rather than to settle the many passages where scholars disagree; where a line is genuinely obscure, the note says so. Readers who want a full scholarly translation should turn to Eccles and Lieu's annotated edition (SERICA, 2016), or to the older versions of Legge (1888) and Saeki (1916).

Note on the text

The inscription is taken from the Taishō Tripiṭaka, vol. 54, no. 2144 (CBETA electronic edition). The original stele has no punctuation; punctuation and paragraphing are this book's. A few evident errors in the base text have been corrected against rubbings of the stone and Eccles and Lieu's 2016 SERICA edition, as follows:

  • "阿罗诃欤判。十字" is repunctuated as "阿罗诃欤！判十字";
  • "总玄摳": the stone has 摳, read as 樞 ("pivot");
  • in "宗周德喪," the base text has a variant form of 喪, here given in its standard form;
  • "腾淩激曰" is corrected to "腾淩激日";
  • "大耀森文曰" is corrected to "大耀森文日";
  • the end of the hymn, "道惟广兮应惟密强。名言兮演三一," is repunctuated as "道惟广兮应惟密，强名言兮演三一";
  • the monk's name "延和" on the side of the stele, missing in the base text, is supplied from the Syriac.

The Chinese is given in traditional characters; characters lost from the stone are shown as □.

Head of the stele and title

OriginalTranslation
〔碑额〕大秦景教流行中國碑[Head] The Stele of the Propagation of the Luminous Religion of Daqin in the Middle Kingdom
Nine characters in regular script, in three columns; above them a cross held up by lotus and clouds.
景教流行中國碑頌并序
大秦寺僧景淨述		A hymn, with preface, on the stele of the propagation of the Luminous Religion in the Middle Kingdom.
Composed by Jingjing, monk of the Daqin monastery.
The "hymn" is the verse that comes at the end; the "preface" is the long prose that comes before it.
SyriacʾĀdām qaššīšā w-kōrappīsqōpā w-pāpaš d-ṢīnistānAdam, priest and chorepiscopus, and papash of Sinistan (China).
Carved beside the author's name, it shows that Jingjing's church name was Adam. The word pāpaš is rare; it is usually taken as an honorific for the head of an ecclesiastical province.

1. Preface: the true Lord, creation, and the fall

OriginalTranslation
粵若：常然真寂，先先而無元；窅然靈虛，後後而妙有。總玄摳而造化，妙衆聖以元尊者，其唯我三一妙身無元真主阿羅訶歟！Behold: the Eternal, true and still, before all firsts and Himself without beginning; the Unfathomable, spirit and void, after all lasts and wondrously enduring; who holds the hidden pivot and makes and transforms all things, who gives the holy ones their wonder and is honoured by them as the Origin—who is this but our Triune Wonder, the beginningless true Lord, Alaha!
Alaha: transliteration of Syriac Alāhā, God. The stone writes 摳; most editions read it as 樞, "pivot."
判十字以定四方，鼓元風而生二氣。暗空易而天地開，日月運而晝夜作。匠成萬物，然立初人，別賜良和，令鎮化海。He set out the cross to fix the four quarters; He stirred the primal wind and brought forth the two breaths. The dark void was changed, and heaven and earth were opened; sun and moon began to move, and day and night were made. With a craftsman's hand He made the ten thousand things, then set up the first man, and gave him besides a nature good and harmonious, and charged him to keep the world He had made.
Primal wind: usually taken as the Holy Spirit (cf. Genesis 1:2, "the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters"). The two breaths are the yin and yang of Chinese cosmology.
渾元之性，虛而不盈；素蕩之心，本無希嗜。Man's first nature, whole and simple, was open and did not swell with itself; his heart, plain and unclouded, had at first no craving.
洎乎娑殫施妄，鈿飾純精，間平大於此是之中，隙冥同於彼非之內。Then Satan worked his falsehood and gilded what was pure and simple; into what men called right he drove a wedge between peace and greatness, and into what they called wrong he secretly blended the two.
Satan: 娑殫 transliterates Syriac Sāṭānā. This sentence is very difficult, and editors punctuate and gloss it in many different ways; its gist is that Satan's deceit turned right and wrong upside down and robbed man of his first harmony.
是以三百六十五種，肩隨結轍，競織法羅。或指物以託宗，或空有以淪二，或禱祀以邀福，或伐善以矯人。智慮營營，恩情役役，茫然無得，煎迫轉燒。積昧亡途，久迷休復。So three hundred and sixty-five sects arose, shoulder to shoulder, rut upon rut, each striving to weave its own net of doctrine. Some pointed to things and made them their gods; some sank into duality over emptiness and being; some prayed and sacrificed to win good fortune; some boasted of their goodness to lord it over others. Their minds toiled without rest, their passions drove them like slaves; they were lost and gained nothing, and the fire that pressed on them burned ever hotter. Their darkness piled up until they lost the road, and they wandered so long that they could not turn back.
"Pointed to things" is usually taken as idolatry or nature worship; "emptiness and being" as the Buddhist debate on śūnyatā and existence, together with the Manichaean doctrine of the two principles; "prayed and sacrificed" as sacrificial cults; "boasted of their goodness" perhaps as those proud of their own good works.

2. Preface: the birth of the Messiah and redemption

OriginalTranslation
於是我三一分身景尊彌施訶，戢隱真威，同人出代。神天宣慶，室女誕聖於大秦；景宿告祥，波斯睹耀以來貢。Thereupon one Person of our Trinity, the Luminous Lord Mishihe, folded away His true majesty and came into the world in the likeness of men. An angel proclaimed the joy; a virgin bore the Holy One in Daqin. A luminous star announced the blessing; Persians saw its brightness and came with tribute.
Mishihe: Syriac Mšīḥā, the Messiah, Christ. "Persians saw its brightness" refers to the Magi (Matthew 2).
圓二十四聖有說之舊法，理家國於大猷；設三一淨風無言之新教，陶良用於正信。He fulfilled the old law spoken by the twenty-four sages, to order house and state by the great plan; He set up the new teaching of the Triune Pure Wind, which is beyond words, to shape the good in man by right faith.
Twenty-four sages: usually taken as the Old Testament prophets (or the twenty-four books of the Hebrew Bible). Pure Wind: the Holy Spirit.
制八境之度，鍊塵成真；啟三常之門，開生滅死。He laid down the measure of the eight conditions, to refine the dust of the world into truth; He opened the gate of the three constants, opening life and destroying death.
Eight conditions: perhaps the eight Beatitudes (Matthew 5), or eight virtues. Three constants: perhaps faith, hope and love; Lin Wushu argues the term is borrowed from Manichaean usage.
懸景日以破暗府，魔妄於是乎悉摧；棹慈航以登明宮，含靈於是乎既濟。能事斯畢，亭午昇真。He hung up the luminous sun to break open the dark realm, and the devil's lies were all destroyed; He rowed the boat of mercy up to the palace of light, and all that has a soul was brought across. His work being done, at high noon He ascended to the Truth.
This passage speaks only of redemption and ascension and does not mention the Crucifixion outright—a point much discussed ever since.

3. Preface: scriptures, rites and rules

OriginalTranslation
經留二十七部，張元化以發靈關；法浴水風，滌浮華而潔虛白。He left twenty-seven books of scripture, to spread the first teaching and open the gate of the spirit; the bath of the Law, by water and wind, washes away vain show and makes the heart clean and clear.
Twenty-seven books: the New Testament. Bath of the Law: baptism.
印持十字，融四照以合無拘；擊木震仁惠之音，東禮趣生榮之路。The cross is their seal; it gathers the light of the four quarters and joins all without hindrance. They strike the wood, and the sound of kindness and mercy rings out; they worship toward the east, and hasten on the road to life and glory.
Strike the wood: the Church of the East called its people to prayer by beating a wooden board (nāqōšā). Worship toward the east: prayer facing east.
存鬚所以有外行，削頂所以無內情。不畜臧獲，均貴賤於人；不聚貨財，示罄遺於我。They keep their beards, to show that they have outward conduct; they shave the crown, to show that they have no inward passions. They keep no slaves, and hold high and low as equal among men; they amass no goods, and show that they have given all away.
Shave the crown: the tonsure of the clergy of the Church of the East.
齋以伏識而成，戒以靜慎為固。七時禮讚，大庇存亡；七日一薦，洗心反素。Their fasting is made perfect by subduing the self; their rule is made firm by stillness and care. Seven times a day they offer praise, and pray widely for the living and the dead; once in seven days they make the offering, to wash the heart and return to simplicity.
Seven times: the seven daily hours of prayer. Once in seven days: the Sunday Eucharist.
真常之道，妙而難名；功用昭彰，強稱景教。The true and constant Way is wondrous and hard to name; its works shine so plainly that we are forced to call it the Luminous Teaching.
Jing (景): light, brightness, greatness. The turn of phrase imitates the Laozi: "I do not know its name; I style it the Way, and, forced to name it, call it great."
惟道非聖不弘，聖非道不大；道聖符契，天下文明。But the Way is not spread abroad without a sage; the sage is not great without the Way. When the Way and the sage fit together like the two halves of a tally, the whole world is enlightened.
The hinge of the whole text: from here it turns to the history of how the "sage" (the Tang emperors) and the "Way" (the Luminous Teaching) prospered together.

4. Preface: the reign of Taizong—Alopen comes to China, and the edict of 638

OriginalTranslation
太宗文皇帝，光華啟運，明聖臨人。大秦國有上德曰阿羅本，占青雲而載真經，望風律以馳艱險。貞觀九祀，至於長安。The Emperor Taizong, the Cultured, opened the dynasty's fortunes in splendour and ruled the people with bright wisdom. In the kingdom of Daqin there was a man of high virtue named Alopen. Reading the omens of the azure clouds, he carried the true scriptures; watching the winds and the pitch-pipes, he hastened through hardship and danger. In the ninth year of Zhenguan he arrived in Chang'an.
"Azure clouds" and "winds and pitch-pipes" allude to the old tradition of envoys from the Western Regions who "watched the wind and came" (cf. the *Records of the Ten Continents* attributed to Dongfang Shuo). The ninth year of Zhenguan is 635.
帝使宰臣房公玄齡總仗西郊，賓迎入內。翻經書殿，問道禁闈。深知正真，特令傳授。The emperor sent his chief minister, Duke Fang Xuanling, with the full honour guard to the western outskirts, to receive him as a guest and bring him in. His scriptures were translated in the library; his Way was inquired into within the palace. The emperor, knowing it deeply to be right and true, gave special command that it be taught.
On the rank of this reception, see Appendix 5.
貞觀十有二年秋七月，詔曰：「道無常名，聖無常體。隨方設教，密濟群生。大秦國大德阿羅本，遠將經像，來獻上京。詳其教旨，玄妙無為；觀其元宗，生成立要。詞無繁說，理有忘筌。濟物利人，宜行天下。」所司即於京義寧坊造大秦寺一所，度僧二十一人。In the autumn, in the seventh month of the twelfth year of Zhenguan, an edict said: "The Way has no constant name; the sage has no constant form. Teachings are set up to suit each place, and quietly save the multitudes. Alopen, a man of great virtue from the kingdom of Daqin, has brought scriptures and images from afar and presented them at the capital. Examining the purport of his teaching, we find it profound and free of striving; looking at its root, it is about giving life and establishing what is essential. Its words are not prolix; its reasoning, once grasped, can do without the trap. It benefits creatures and profits men, and ought to be practised throughout the world." The office concerned at once built a Daqin monastery in the Yining Ward of the capital and ordained twenty-one monks.
The same edict as recorded in the *Tang Huiyao*, chapter 49, reads "the Persian monk Alopen brought scriptures and teaching from afar" and "build a monastery in the Yining Ward," with no mention of "Daqin"; only in the fourth year of Tianbao (745) were the "Persian monasteries" renamed "Daqin monasteries." The stele uses the name current in 781. "Can do without the trap" alludes to the Zhuangzi: once the fish is caught, the fish-trap is forgotten. The twelfth year of Zhenguan is 638.
宗周德喪，青駕西昇；巨唐道光，景風東扇。旋令有司，將帝寫真轉摸寺壁。天姿汎彩，英朗景門；聖迹騰祥，永輝法界。When the virtue of Zhou declined, the blue chariot ascended into the west; now that the Way of the great Tang shines, the Luminous wind blows east. Soon the emperor ordered the officials to copy his portrait onto the monastery wall. The heavenly countenance spread its colours and lit the Luminous gate; the sacred likeness sent up good omens and shines for ever on the realm of the Law.
The base text has a variant form of 喪, here given in its standard form. "The blue chariot" is Laozi, who by legend left China riding a blue ox westward through the pass; it is set against "the Luminous wind blows east."

5. Preface: the land of Daqin

OriginalTranslation
案《西域圖記》及漢魏史策，大秦國南統珊瑚之海，北極衆寶之山，西望仙境花林，東接長風弱水。其土出火浣布、返魂香、明月珠、夜光璧。俗無寇盜，人有樂康。法非景不行，主非德不立。土宇廣闊，文物昌明。According to the *Illustrated Record of the Western Regions* and the histories of the Han and Wei, the kingdom of Daqin rules the Sea of Coral to the south and reaches the Mountains of All Treasures to the north; to the west it looks toward the flowering forests of the immortals, and to the east it borders the Long Winds and the Weak Waters. Its soil yields fire-washed cloth, incense that calls back the soul, bright-moon pearls and night-shining jade. Its people know no robbers; they live in happiness and ease. No law is followed there but the Luminous; no ruler is set up but the virtuous. Its lands are broad, its culture flourishing.
The *Illustrated Record of the Western Regions* was written by Pei Ju of the Sui. This passage is drawn almost entirely from the legends of Daqin in the *Book of the Later Han* and the *Weilüe*, not from anything seen at first hand. Fire-washed cloth is asbestos cloth, cleaned by putting it in the fire.

6. Preface: from Gaozong to Xuanzong

OriginalTranslation
高宗大帝，克恭纘祖，潤色真宗，而於諸州各置景寺，仍崇阿羅本為鎮國大法主。法流十道，國富元休；寺滿百城，家殷景福。The Great Emperor Gaozong reverently continued his ancestor's work and added lustre to the true religion. He set up Luminous monasteries in every prefecture and honoured Alopen as Great Lord of the Law, Guardian of the State. The Law flowed through the ten circuits, and the state was rich in peace; monasteries filled a hundred cities, and every house abounded in Luminous blessing.
"Ten circuits" and "a hundred cities" are generally regarded as rhetorical exaggeration; the actual number of Luminous monasteries was small (Lin Wushu has a study of the numbers on the stele).
聖曆年，釋子用壯，騰口於東周；先天末，下士大笑，訕謗於西鎬。In the Shengli years the sons of Śākya used their strength and raised their voices in Eastern Zhou; at the end of Xiantian, lesser scholars laughed aloud and slandered us in Western Hao.
Shengli: 698–700; Xiantian: 712–713. Eastern Zhou is Luoyang, Western Hao is Chang'an. "Lesser scholars laughed aloud" quotes the Laozi—"when the lesser scholar hears the Way, he laughs aloud"—and is usually taken to mean the Daoists.
有若僧首羅含、大德及烈，並金方貴緒，物外高僧，共振玄網，俱維絕紐。Then there were the chief monk Luohan and the Great Virtue Jilie, both of noble lineage from the land of metal, eminent monks beyond the world, who together drew taut the hidden net again and bound up the cord that had broken.
Land of metal: the west (in the five phases, the west belongs to metal).
玄宗至道皇帝，令寧國等五王親臨福宇，建立壇場。法棟暫橈而更崇，道石時傾而復正。The Emperor Xuanzong, Perfect in the Way, ordered the Prince of Ning and four other princes to go in person to the house of blessing and set up an altar there. The beam of the Law, bent for a while, rose higher; the stone of the Way, tilted for a time, stood upright again.
天寶初，令大將軍高力士送五聖寫真寺內安置，賜絹百匹，奉慶睿圖。龍髯雖遠，弓劍可攀；日角舒光，天顏咫尺。At the beginning of Tianbao he ordered the General-in-Chief Gao Lishi to take the portraits of the five sage emperors to the monastery and set them up there, and gave a hundred bolts of silk, in celebration of the sacred likenesses. Though the dragon's beard is far away, the bow and sword may still be grasped; the brow like the sun spreads its light, and the heavenly face is a foot away.
The five sages: Gaozu, Taizong, Gaozong, Zhongzong and Ruizong. "The dragon's beard" and "the bow and sword" allude to the legend that when the Yellow Emperor rode a dragon up to heaven, his ministers clung to the dragon's beard, and his bow and sword fell to earth (*Shiji*, "Treatise on the Feng and Shan Sacrifices").
三載，大秦國有僧佶和，瞻星向化，望日朝尊。詔僧羅含、僧普論等一七人，與大德佶和，於興慶宮修功德。In the third year, there was a monk of Daqin named Jihe, who looked to the stars and turned toward our civilisation, and gazed at the sun and came to pay homage to the Most High. An edict summoned the monk Luohan, the monk Pulun and others, seventeen in all, with the Great Virtue Jihe, to perform rites of merit in the Xingqing Palace.
The third year of Tianbao is 744. 一七人 may also be read "seven persons." The Xingqing Palace was Xuanzong's chief residence; "rites of merit" are religious services held to bring blessing on the imperial house.
於是天題寺牓，額戴龍書。寶裝璀翠，灼爍丹霞；睿扎宏空，騰淩激日。寵賚比南山峻極，沛澤與東海齊深。Thereupon the emperor himself wrote the monastery's name-board, and the board bore the dragon's script. Its jewelled adornment glittered blue-green and blazed like red clouds at dawn; the imperial brush filled the sky, soaring and flashing against the sun. His gifts were as high as the Southern Mountains, his bounty as deep as the Eastern Sea.
激日: the *Taishō* misprints it as 激曰.
道無不可，所可可名；聖無不作，所作可述。There is nothing the Way cannot do, and what it does can be named; there is nothing the sage does not make, and what he makes can be recorded.

7. Preface: the reigns of Suzong, Daizong and Dezong

OriginalTranslation
肅宗文明皇帝，於靈武等五郡重立景寺。元善資而福祚開，大慶臨而皇業建。The Emperor Suzong, Cultured and Bright, rebuilt Luminous monasteries in Lingwu and four other commanderies. The great good was aided, and the dynasty's fortune opened; great joy came down, and the imperial work was founded anew.
Suzong took the throne at Lingwu in 756. "Rebuilt" suggests that Luminous monasteries had suffered in the An Lushan rebellion.
代宗文武皇帝，恢張聖運，從事無為。每於降誕之辰，錫天香以告成功，頒御饌以光景衆。The Emperor Daizong, Cultured and Martial, enlarged the sacred fortunes of the dynasty and governed without striving. Every year, at the time of the Nativity, he sent heavenly incense to announce his success, and gave dishes from the imperial table to honour the Luminous congregation.
"The time of the Nativity" can be read two ways: Daizong's own birthday, or Christmas.
且乾以美利故能廣生，聖以體元故能亭毒。Now Heaven, through its fair benefits, can give life broadly; the sage, through embodying the origin, can nourish and bring to maturity.
"Heaven through its fair benefits" is from the commentary on the hexagram *Qian* in the *Book of Changes*; "nourish and bring to maturity" (亭毒) from Laozi 51.
我建中聖神文武皇帝，披八政以黜陟幽明，闡九疇以惟新景命。化通玄理，祝無愧心。Our Emperor of Jianzhong, Sage, Divine, Cultured and Martial, unfolds the eight branches of government to demote the dull and promote the bright, and sets forth the nine categories to renew the luminous mandate. His teaching reaches the deep principles; his prayers are offered with an unashamed heart.
The eight branches and nine categories come from the "Great Plan" chapter of the *Book of Documents*. The emperor is Dezong.
至於方大而虛，專靜而恕，廣慈救衆苦，善貸被群生者，我修行之大猷，汲引之階漸也。To be upright and great yet humble, single-minded and still yet forgiving, to save the many from suffering with a wide compassion, to give freely so that all living things are covered—this is the great plan of our practice, the ladder by which we draw others up.
若使風雨時，天下靜，人能理，物能清，存能昌，歿能樂，念生響應，情發目誠者，我景力能事之功用也。That wind and rain should come in their season, the world be at peace, men be orderly and things be pure, the living prosper and the dead be at rest, that every thought should meet its answer and every feeling come from a true heart—this is the work the Luminous power can do.

8. Preface: the great benefactor Yisi

OriginalTranslation
大施主金紫光祿大夫、同朔方節度副使、試殿中監、賜紫袈裟僧伊斯，和而好惠，聞道勤行。遠自王舍之城，聿來中夏。The great benefactor, the monk Yisi—Grand Master of the Golden Seal and Purple Ribbon, Associate Deputy Military Governor of Shuofang, Acting Director of the Palace Administration, granted the purple robe—was gentle and loved to do kindness; having heard the Way, he practised it diligently. From the distant city of Rajagriha he came to the Middle Kingdom.
Rajagriha: according to the Syriac at the foot of the stele, this is Balkh in Tokharistan (in northern Afghanistan today). Xuanzang's *Record of the Western Regions* calls Balkh "Little Rajagriha." For a line-by-line study, see Appendix 3.
術高三代，藝博十全。始効節於丹庭，乃策名於王帳。His art surpassed the three generations; his skill was broad and wholly sure. He first served loyally at the vermilion court, and then enrolled his name in the royal tent.
"Three generations" and "wholly sure" are usually read as medical terms: the *Book of Rites* says "do not take medicine from a physician whose family has not practised for three generations," and the *Rites of Zhou* rates a physician who cures ten out of ten as the best.
中書令汾陽郡王郭公子儀，初總戎於朔方也，肅宗俾之從邁。雖見親於臥內，不自異於行間。為公爪牙，作軍耳目。When Lord Guo Ziyi, President of the Secretariat and Prince of Fenyang, first took command of the army in Shuofang, Suzong had Yisi go with him on campaign. Though he was trusted even in the bedchamber, he did not set himself apart in the ranks. He served the Duke as claws and teeth, and the army as eyes and ears.
能散祿賜，不積於家。獻臨恩之頗黎，布辭憩之金罽。He could give away his salary and rewards, and did not hoard them at home. He offered up the glassware he received by grace, and spread out the gold-threaded carpets given him on retiring.
頗黎: glass or crystal. 金罽: carpets woven with gold thread. Scholars differ on the meaning of 臨恩 and 辭憩.
或仍其舊寺，或重廣法堂，崇飾廊宇，如翬斯飛。Some old monasteries he restored as they were; some halls of the Law he enlarged anew. He raised and adorned their colonnades and roofs, until they were like a pheasant in flight.
"Like a pheasant in flight" is from the *Book of Songs* ("Si Gan"), describing upswept eaves.
更効景門，依仁施利。每歲集四寺僧徒，虔事精供，備諸五旬。He served the Luminous gate still more, and gave benefits in accordance with benevolence. Every year he gathered the monks of four monasteries, and served them reverently and provided for them carefully for fifty days.
五旬, fifty days: perhaps the great fast of the Church of the East, or the fifty days after Easter.
餧者來而飯之，寒者來而衣之，病者療而起之，死者葬而安之。The hungry came and were fed; the cold came and were clothed; the sick were healed and raised up; the dead were buried and laid to rest.
Cf. Matthew 25:35–36. 餧 is a form of 馁, "hungry."
清節達娑，未聞斯美；白衣景士，今見其人。願刻洪碑，以揚休烈。Among the pure and upright tarsa, such goodness was never heard of; among the white-robed Luminous scholars, now we see the man. We wish to carve a great stele, to make his noble deeds known.
Tarsa: transliteration of Middle Iranian tarsā, "one who fears (God)," a term for Christians.

9. The hymn

OriginalTranslation
詞曰：
真主無元，湛寂常然；權輿匠化，起地立天。
分身出代，救度無邊；日昇暗滅，咸證真玄。		The words say:
The true Lord is without beginning, deep and still for ever; He began the work of making, raised the earth and set up the heavens.
One Person came into the world, to save without limit; the sun rose, the darkness perished, and all bore witness to the deep truth.
赫赫文皇，道冠前王；乘時撥亂，乾廓坤張。
明明景教，言歸我唐；翻經建寺，存歿舟航。
百福偕作，萬邦之康。		Glorious was the Cultured Emperor, his Way above all kings before; he seized the hour and put down disorder, and heaven and earth were opened wide.
Bright, bright, the Luminous Teaching came home to our Tang; scriptures were translated, monasteries built, a ferry for the living and the dead.
A hundred blessings arose together; ten thousand states had peace.
高宗纂祖，更築精宇；和宮敞朗，遍滿中土。
真道宣明，式封法主；人有樂康，物無災苦。		Gaozong carried on his forebear's work and built more fine halls; palaces of harmony, wide and bright, filled the Middle Land.
The true Way was proclaimed and made clear; a Lord of the Law was appointed; the people had happiness and ease, and creatures knew no calamity or pain.
玄宗啟聖，克修真正；御牓揚輝，天書蔚映。
皇圖璀璨，率土高敬；庶績咸熙，人賴其慶。		Xuanzong opened the way of the sage and cultivated what is true and right; his name-board shone forth, his heavenly script gleamed.
The imperial portraits glittered, and all the land revered them; every labour prospered, and the people relied on his blessing.
肅宗來復，天威引駕；聖日舒晶，祥風掃夜。
祚歸皇室，祅氛永謝；止沸定塵，造我區夏。		Suzong came back to restore the realm, heaven's might leading his chariot; the holy sun spread its brightness, an auspicious wind swept away the night.
The throne returned to the royal house, the baleful vapours were banished for ever; he stilled the boiling and settled the dust, and made our China anew.
"Baleful vapours" means the An Lushan and Shi Siming rebellion.
代宗孝義，德合天地；開貸生成，物資美利。
香以報功，仁以作施；暘谷來威，月窟畢萃。		Daizong was filial and just, his virtue at one with heaven and earth; he opened his bounty and brought things to life, and all creatures had fair benefit.
With incense he repaid merit, with kindness he gave; the Valley of the Dawn came in awe, and the Cave of the Moon gathered in full.
The Valley of the Dawn and the Cave of the Moon are the mythical farthest east and farthest west.
建中統極，聿修明德；武肅四溟，文清萬域。
燭臨人隱，鏡觀物色；六合昭蘇，百蠻取則。		The Emperor of Jianzhong took the highest seat and cultivated bright virtue; his arms quelled the four seas, his culture cleansed ten thousand lands.
Like a candle he lights the hidden griefs of men, like a mirror he sees the face of things; the six directions revive, and a hundred barbarian peoples take him as their pattern.
道惟廣兮應惟密，強名言兮演三一。
主能作兮臣能述，建豐碑兮頌元吉。		How broad is the Way, how fine its answering! Forced to give it a name, we set forth the Three in One.
The lord can act, the subject can record; we raise this great stele to praise the supreme good.
The *Taishō* punctuates this as "道惟廣兮應惟密強。名言兮演三一," which is wrong; it is corrected here.

10. Date of erection, the calligrapher, and names at the foot

OriginalTranslation
大唐建中二年，歲在作噩，太蔟月七日，大耀森文日建立。時法主僧寧恕，知東方之景衆也。Erected in the second year of Jianzhong of the Great Tang, the year being *zuo'e*, on the seventh day of the month of *taicou*, a great *yaosenwen* day. At that time the Lord of the Law, the monk Ningshu, oversaw the Luminous congregations of the East.
The seventh day of the first month of the second year of Jianzhong corresponds to 4 February 781 in the Julian calendar, which was indeed a Sunday. *Yaosenwen* transliterates the Middle Persian for "first day of the week." *Zuo'e* is the year-name for a *xinyou* year; *taicou* is a name for the first month. Ningshu is the Patriarch Ḥnanīšōʿ II (Hnanisho); he had in fact already died when the stele was raised, but the news had not yet reached Chang'an. 曰 is a misprint in the *Taishō*, here corrected to 日.
SyriacB-yawmay abā d-abāhātā Mār(y) Ḥnanīšōʿ qātōlīqā paṭriyarkīsIn the days of the Father of fathers, Mar Hnanisho, Catholicos and Patriarch.
The Syriac dating formula, matching the Chinese "at that time the Lord of the Law, the monk Ningshu."
朝議郎前行台州司士參軍呂秀巖書。Written by Lü Xiuyan, Gentleman for Court Discussion, formerly Administrator of Granaries in Taizhou.
Lü Xiuyan wrote the inscription out for carving and also the title on the head. An old tradition identifying him with the immortal Lü Dongbin is not to be trusted.
SyriacB-šnat ʾalp w-tišʿīn w-trēn d-Yawnāyē, Mār(y) Yazdbōzīd qaššīšā w-kōrappīsqōpā d-Kumdān mdīttā d-malkūtā, bar nīḥ napšā Mīlīs qaššīšā d-men Balḫ mdīttā d-Ṭaḥūrisṭān, ʾaqīm lūḥā hānā d-kēpā ...In the year one thousand and ninety-two of the Greeks (780/781), Mar Yazdbozid, priest and chorepiscopus of Kumdan, the royal city (Chang'an), son of the late priest Milis, from Balkh, a city of Tokharistan, set up this tablet of stone, on which is written the way of our Saviour's salvation, and the preaching of our fathers to the kings of the Chinese.
The main Syriac inscription at the foot of the stele (transliteration abridged). The Chinese text calls Yisi only "the great benefactor"; the Syriac says plainly that it was he who raised the stele.
SyriacʾĀdām mšammšānā bar Yazdbōzīd kōrappīsqōpā · Mār(y) Sargīs qaššīšā w-kōrappīsqōpā · Ṣabrānīšōʿ qaššīšā · Gabrīʾēl qaššīšā w-ʾarkīdyaqōn w-rēš ʿēdtā d-Kumdān w-d-SaragAdam, deacon, son of Yazdbozid the chorepiscopus. Mar Sargis, priest and chorepiscopus. Sabranisho, priest. Gabriel, priest and archdeacon, head of the church of Kumdan and of Sarag (Chang'an and Luoyang).
The deacon Adam is the one named "Lingbao" in Chinese on the side of the stele: Yisi's son. He shares the name Adam with Jingjing, the author, but is a different person. Sabranisho is "Xingtong."
助撿挍試太常卿賜紫袈裟寺主僧業利
撿挍建立碑僧行通		Assisting in supervising: the monk Yeli, Acting Chief Minister of the Court of Imperial Sacrifices, granted the purple robe, head of the monastery.
Supervising the erection of the stele: the monk Xingtong.
撿挍 is 檢校, "to supervise." "Head of the monastery" corresponds to the Syriac "head of the church," and Yeli is generally identified with Gabriel.

12. A later inscription

OriginalTranslation
後一千七十九年，咸豐己未，武林韓泰華來觀，幸字畫完整，重造碑亭覆焉。惜故友吳子苾方伯不及同遊也，為悵然久之。One thousand and seventy-nine years later, in the *jiwei* year of Xianfeng, Han Taihua of Wulin came to see the stele. Glad to find its characters intact, he had a new pavilion built to cover it. He regretted that his late friend, Provincial Treasurer Wu Zibi, could not have come with him, and was melancholy over it for a long while.
The *jiwei* year of Xianfeng is 1859; Wulin is Hangzhou. This colophon was cut on the side of the stele in the Qing and is not part of the Tang original.
